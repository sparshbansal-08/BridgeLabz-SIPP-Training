1. Introduction and Self Presentation

1. Tell me about yourself.
Start with your name, education, and key skills, then mention a recent project and your career interest. Example: "I am a computer science graduate skilled in Java, Spring Boot, and SQL. In my last project I built a web application using Spring MVC and MySQL. I enjoy backend development and want to grow as a full-stack developer."
2. Why did you choose software development?
I enjoy solving problems logically and seeing my code turn into a working product that people use. Software development lets me keep learning new technologies continuously.
3. What are your key technical skills?
Core Java, OOP, Spring MVC, Spring Boot, REST APIs, SQL/MySQL, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Git, and basic knowledge of testing with JUnit. Tailor this list to the job description.
4. What are your strengths?
Quick learner, strong problem-solving ability, good teamwork, and attention to detail. Always back a strength with a short real example.
5. What is your weakness?
Pick a genuine but non-critical weakness and show improvement. Example: "I used to hesitate asking for help, but I now reach out early, which saves time for the whole team."
6. Where do you see yourself in five years?
I see myself as an experienced developer who has grown into a senior or lead role, mentoring juniors and contributing to important technical decisions.
7. Why should we hire you?
I have the required technical skills, I learn quickly, and I am a reliable team player. I am confident I can contribute value and grow with the company.
8. What motivates you?
Solving challenging problems, learning new technologies, and seeing my work create real impact for users.
9. How do you handle pressure and deadlines?
I break the work into smaller tasks, prioritize the most important ones, and communicate early if I need help. Staying organized keeps me calm under pressure.
10. Do you prefer working in a team or alone?
I am comfortable with both. I enjoy team collaboration for sharing ideas, and I can also work independently when a task needs focus.
11. How do you keep your skills updated?
I follow technical blogs and documentation, take online courses, build small practice projects, and learn from code reviews.
12. What do you know about our company?
Research the company before the interview. Mention its products, services, technology stack, and values, and connect them to your interest.
13. Briefly describe your most recent project.
State the goal, your role, the technologies used, and the outcome. Keep it to a few sentences and be ready for follow-up questions.
14. What is your biggest achievement?
Pick one concrete accomplishment, such as delivering a project on time or solving a tough bug, and explain the impact it had.
15. How do you handle feedback or criticism?
I see feedback as a chance to improve. I listen carefully, ask questions if needed, and apply the suggestions in my work.
16. What are your hobbies?
Mention genuine hobbies that show positive traits, such as reading, sports, or coding side projects.
17. Why do you want to start your career in the IT industry?
I enjoy technology and problem-solving, and the IT industry lets me keep learning and build solutions that create real impact. It matches my skills and long-term interest.
18. How do you quickly learn a new technology or tool?
I start with the official documentation and tutorials, build a small practice example, and apply it to a real task step by step. I also ask seniors or refer to trusted resources when stuck.
19. What kind of role are you looking for?
A role where I can apply my Java and web development skills, learn from experienced colleagues, and grow technically as a developer.
20. How do you prioritize your work?
I list my tasks, judge them by urgency and importance, handle high-priority items first, and keep my team informed of progress.
21. Do you have any questions for us?
Always ask one or two thoughtful questions, such as about the training program, team structure, technology stack, or growth opportunities. It shows genuine interest.

2. OOP Concepts

1. What is Object-Oriented Programming?
OOP is a way of designing software around objects that combine data (fields) and behavior (methods). It makes code modular, reusable, and easier to maintain.
2. What are the four pillars of OOP?
Encapsulation, Inheritance, Polymorphism, and Abstraction.
3. What is a class and an object?
A class is a blueprint that defines fields and methods. An object is a real instance created from that class. Example: Car is a class; myCar is an object.
4. What is encapsulation?
Wrapping data and methods together and hiding internal details. We make fields private and expose them through public getters and setters.
5. What is inheritance?
A mechanism where one class (child) acquires the fields and methods of another class (parent) using the extends keyword. It promotes code reuse.
6. What is polymorphism?
The ability of one action to behave differently based on the object. It comes in two forms: compile-time (overloading) and runtime (overriding).
7. What is the difference between compile-time and runtime polymorphism?
Compile-time polymorphism is achieved by method overloading and resolved by the compiler. Runtime polymorphism is achieved by method overriding and resolved during execution.
8. What is the difference between overloading and overriding?
  • Overloading: same method name, different parameters, within one class.
  • Overriding: subclass redefines a parent method with the same signature.
9. What is abstraction?
Hiding complex implementation and showing only the essential features. Achieved using abstract classes and interfaces.
10. What is the difference between an abstract class and an interface?
  • Abstract class: can have both abstract and concrete methods, fields, and constructors; a class extends only one.
  • Interface: defines a contract; from Java 8 it can have default and static methods; a class can implement many.
11. What is a constructor?
A special method with the same name as the class, used to initialize objects. It has no return type and runs automatically when an object is created.
12. What is constructor overloading?
Defining multiple constructors in the same class with different parameter lists to create objects in different ways.
13. What does the this keyword do?
It refers to the current object. It is used to access the current object's fields and methods, often to resolve naming conflicts.
14. What does the super keyword do?
It refers to the parent class. It is used to call the parent's constructor or access parent methods and fields.
15. What does the static keyword mean?
Static members belong to the class rather than any object. They are shared across all instances and can be accessed without creating an object.
16. What does the final keyword mean?
  • final variable: value cannot be changed.
  • final method: cannot be overridden.
  • final class: cannot be inherited.
17. What are association, aggregation, and composition?
All describe relationships between objects. Association is a general "uses" link. Aggregation is a weak whole-part where parts can exist alone. Composition is a strong whole-part where parts cannot exist without the whole.
18. What is coupling and cohesion?
Coupling is the degree of dependency between classes (low is better). Cohesion is how focused a class is on a single task (high is better).
19. Can you override a static method?
No. Static methods belong to the class and are not polymorphic. Redeclaring one in a subclass is method hiding, not overriding.
20. What is the difference between is-a and has-a relationships?
"Is-a" represents inheritance (a Dog is an Animal). "Has-a" represents composition or association (a Car has an Engine).
21. What is the diamond problem?
An ambiguity when a class inherits the same method from two parents. Java avoids it by not allowing multiple class inheritance; interfaces with default methods resolve it explicitly.

3. Collections Framework

1. What is the Collections Framework?
A set of ready-made classes and interfaces in java.util for storing and manipulating groups of objects, such as List, Set, Map, and Queue.
2. What is the difference between Collection and Collections?
Collection is the root interface of the framework. Collections is a utility class with static helper methods like sort() and reverse().
3. What is the difference between List, Set, and Map?
  • List: ordered, allows duplicates.
  • Set: no duplicates.
  • Map: stores key-value pairs with unique keys.
4. What is the difference between ArrayList and LinkedList?
ArrayList uses a dynamic array, fast for random access. LinkedList uses a doubly linked list, faster for frequent insertions and deletions.
5. What is the difference between ArrayList and Vector?
Both are dynamic arrays. Vector is synchronized (thread-safe but slower); ArrayList is not synchronized and is preferred in single-threaded code.
6. What is the difference between HashSet, LinkedHashSet, and TreeSet?
  • HashSet: no order, fastest.
  • LinkedHashSet: maintains insertion order.
  • TreeSet: keeps elements sorted.
7. What is the difference between HashMap and Hashtable?
HashMap is not synchronized and allows one null key; Hashtable is synchronized and allows no null keys or values. HashMap is generally preferred.
8. What is the difference between HashMap, LinkedHashMap, and TreeMap?
HashMap has no order, LinkedHashMap preserves insertion order, and TreeMap keeps keys sorted.
9. How does HashMap work internally?
It stores entries in buckets based on the key's hash code. On collision it chains entries (linked list, converting to a balanced tree after a threshold in modern Java).
10. What is the contract between hashCode and equals?
If two objects are equal by equals(), they must return the same hashCode(). Breaking this contract causes bugs in hash-based collections.
11. What is the difference between fail-fast and fail-safe iterators?
Fail-fast iterators throw ConcurrentModificationException if the collection changes during iteration. Fail-safe iterators (in concurrent collections) work on a copy and do not throw.
12. What is the difference between Iterator and ListIterator?
Iterator moves forward only. ListIterator can move both forward and backward and can add or replace elements.
13. What is the difference between Comparable and Comparator?
Comparable defines a natural ordering through compareTo() inside the class. Comparator defines custom orderings through compare() in a separate class.
14. What is the difference between Queue and Deque?
A Queue processes elements first-in-first-out. A Deque (double-ended queue) allows adding and removing from both ends.
15. What is a PriorityQueue?
A queue where elements are ordered by priority (natural order or a comparator) rather than insertion order.
16. What is the difference between synchronized and concurrent collections?
Synchronized collections lock the whole object per operation. Concurrent collections like ConcurrentHashMap use finer-grained locking for better performance.
17. What is ConcurrentHashMap?
A thread-safe map that allows concurrent reads and segmented writes without locking the entire map, making it efficient in multithreaded code.
18. What are load factor and initial capacity in HashMap?
Initial capacity is the starting number of buckets. Load factor (default 0.75) decides when the map resizes. The map grows when entries exceed capacity times load factor.
19. How do you make a collection read-only?
Wrap it with Collections.unmodifiableList() (or similar), or use List.of() to create an immutable collection.
20. What is the difference between an array and an ArrayList?
An array has a fixed size and can hold primitives. An ArrayList is resizable, holds objects, and provides many built-in methods.
21. What are generics in collections?
Generics let you specify the element type, like List<String>. They provide compile-time type safety and remove the need for casting.

4. Multithreading

1. What is a thread and a process?
A process is an independent running program with its own memory. A thread is a lightweight unit of execution inside a process; multiple threads share the process memory.
2. What are the ways to create a thread in Java?
By extending the Thread class or by implementing the Runnable interface (preferred). You can also use Callable with an ExecutorService.
3. Why is Runnable preferred over extending Thread?
Implementing Runnable keeps your class free to extend another class and separates the task from the thread, which is cleaner and more flexible.
4. What are the states in a thread lifecycle?
New, Runnable, Running, Blocked/Waiting, and Terminated.
5. What is the difference between start() and run()?
start() creates a new thread and then calls run() on it. Calling run() directly executes it on the current thread, with no new thread created.
6. What is the difference between sleep() and wait()?
sleep() pauses a thread for a time and keeps the lock. wait() releases the lock and waits until notified. sleep() is in Thread; wait() is in Object.
7. What is synchronization?
A mechanism that allows only one thread at a time to access a shared resource, preventing data corruption in multithreaded programs.
8. What is the difference between a synchronized method and a synchronized block?
A synchronized method locks the whole method. A synchronized block locks only a chosen section, which is more efficient.
9. What is a deadlock?
A situation where two or more threads are blocked forever, each waiting for a lock held by the other.
10. What is a race condition?
When two threads access shared data at the same time and the final result depends on timing, causing inconsistent output. Synchronization prevents it.
11. What does the volatile keyword do?
It tells the JVM that a variable may change across threads, so each read comes from main memory rather than a cached copy, ensuring visibility.
12. What is a thread pool and ExecutorService?
A thread pool reuses a fixed set of threads for many tasks. ExecutorService manages this pool, avoiding the cost of creating threads repeatedly.
13. What is the difference between Runnable and Callable?
Runnable's run() returns nothing and cannot throw checked exceptions. Callable's call() returns a result and can throw checked exceptions.
14. What is the difference between notify() and notifyAll()?
notify() wakes one waiting thread; notifyAll() wakes all waiting threads, which then compete for the lock.
15. What is a daemon thread?
A background thread (like garbage collection) that does not prevent the JVM from exiting once all user threads finish.
16. What does the join() method do?
It makes the current thread wait until the thread it is called on finishes execution.
17. What is thread safety?
Code is thread-safe when it behaves correctly even when accessed by multiple threads at the same time.
18. What is the difference between synchronized and Lock?
synchronized is simpler and automatic. The Lock interface offers more control, such as try-lock, timed lock, and fairness, but must be released manually.
19. What are atomic variables?
Classes like AtomicInteger that perform thread-safe operations without locks using low-level atomic instructions.
20. What does the yield() method do?
It hints to the scheduler that the current thread is willing to pause so other threads of equal priority can run. It is only a hint.
21. What is the producer-consumer problem?
A classic concurrency problem where producers add items to a shared buffer and consumers remove them, coordinated using wait/notify or a blocking queue.

5. Exception Handling

1. What is an exception?
An unwanted event that disrupts the normal flow of a program at runtime, such as dividing by zero or accessing a null object.
2. What is the difference between an Exception and an Error?
Exceptions are conditions an application can catch and handle. Errors (like OutOfMemoryError) are serious problems that usually cannot be recovered from.
3. What is the difference between checked and unchecked exceptions?
Checked exceptions are checked at compile time and must be handled or declared (e.g., IOException). Unchecked exceptions occur at runtime (e.g., NullPointerException).
4. What is the use of try-catch-finally?
try holds risky code, catch handles the exception, and finally runs cleanup code whether or not an exception occurs.
5. Can the finally block be skipped?
Almost never. It is skipped only if the JVM exits (System.exit()) or the thread is killed.
6. What is the difference between throw and throws?
throw actually throws an exception object. throws declares in the method signature that a method may throw exceptions.
7. Can we have multiple catch blocks?
Yes. A try can have several catch blocks for different exception types, ordered from most specific to most general.
8. What is try-with-resources?
A feature where resources declared in the try(...) are closed automatically, as long as they implement AutoCloseable.
9. How do you create a custom exception?
Create a class that extends Exception (checked) or RuntimeException (unchecked) and provide constructors that pass a message to the parent.
10. What is the exception hierarchy?
Throwable is the root, with two branches: Error and Exception. RuntimeException is a subclass of Exception for unchecked exceptions.
11. What is the difference between finally and finalize?
finally is a block for cleanup after try-catch. finalize() was a method called before garbage collection (now deprecated).
12. Can we have a try without a catch?
Yes, if it is paired with a finally block or used as try-with-resources.
13. What are common causes of NullPointerException?
Calling a method or accessing a field on a null reference, or accessing an element of a null array. Always check or initialize objects first.
14. What are best practices in exception handling?
  • Catch specific exceptions, not just Exception.
  • Never leave a catch block empty.
  • Log exceptions with context and clean up resources.
15. Give examples of checked exceptions.
IOException, SQLException, and FileNotFoundException.
16. Give examples of unchecked exceptions.
NullPointerException, ArrayIndexOutOfBoundsException, and ArithmeticException.
17. What is exception propagation?
If a method does not handle an exception, it passes up the call stack to the caller until it is caught or reaches the JVM.
18. Can a constructor throw an exception?
Yes. A constructor can throw exceptions to signal that an object could not be created in a valid state.
19. What is rethrowing an exception?
Catching an exception and throwing it again, often after logging or wrapping it in a more meaningful exception.
20. What is multi-catch?
A single catch block handling multiple exception types using the pipe symbol, for example catch (IOException | SQLException e).
21. What happens if an exception is not handled?
It propagates up the stack; if never caught, the program terminates and the JVM prints the stack trace.

6. Git

1. What is Git?
Git is a distributed version control system that tracks changes in source code and lets multiple developers collaborate efficiently.
2. What is the difference between Git and GitHub?
Git is the tool that manages versions on your computer. GitHub is an online platform that hosts Git repositories for sharing and collaboration.
3. What is a repository?
A repository (repo) is a project folder tracked by Git, containing all files and the full history of changes.
4. What does git init do?
It creates a new, empty Git repository in the current folder by adding a hidden .git directory.
5. What does git clone do?
It downloads a complete copy of an existing remote repository, including its history, to your local machine.
6. What does git add do?
It moves changes from the working directory to the staging area, marking them to be included in the next commit.
7. What does git commit do?
It permanently records the staged changes in the local repository along with a descriptive message.
8. What is the staging area?
An intermediate area (the index) where you assemble exactly which changes will go into the next commit.
9. What does git push do?
It uploads your local commits to the remote repository so others can see them.
10. What does git pull do?
It downloads changes from the remote and merges them into your current branch. It is a fetch plus a merge.
11. What is the difference between git fetch and git pull?
git fetch only downloads new commits without changing your working branch. git pull downloads and merges them.
12. What is a branch?
A branch is an independent line of development, letting you work on features without affecting the main code.
13. How do you create and switch to a branch?
Use git branch name to create and git checkout name to switch, or do both with git checkout -b name (or git switch -c name).
14. What is merging?
Combining the changes from one branch into another, for example bringing a feature branch into main.
15. What is the difference between merge and rebase?
Merge keeps both histories and creates a merge commit. Rebase replays your commits on top of another branch to create a clean, linear history.
16. What is a merge conflict?
It happens when two branches change the same lines of a file, and Git cannot decide automatically which version to keep.
17. How do you resolve a merge conflict?
Open the conflicted file, choose the correct code, remove the conflict markers, then git add the file and commit (or continue the rebase).
18. What does git status show?
It shows which files are modified, staged, or untracked, and the current branch.
19. What does git log show?
It shows the commit history, including commit IDs, authors, dates, and messages.
20. What is a .gitignore file?
A file that lists patterns for files and folders Git should ignore, such as build outputs, logs, and IDE settings.
21. What is the difference between git revert and git reset?
git revert creates a new commit that undoes a previous one, keeping history safe. git reset moves the branch pointer back and can rewrite history.

7. HTML

1. What is HTML?
HTML (HyperText Markup Language) is the standard language used to create the structure and content of web pages using elements and tags.
2. What is the difference between HTML and HTML5?
HTML5 is the latest version. It adds semantic tags, audio and video support, canvas, local storage, and new form input types.
3. What is the basic structure of an HTML document?
It starts with <!DOCTYPE html>, then <html> containing a <head> (metadata) and a <body> (visible content).
4. What is the difference between block and inline elements?
Block elements (like <div>, <p>) start on a new line and take full width. Inline elements (like <span>, <a>) sit within a line.
5. What are semantic elements?
Tags that describe their meaning, such as <header>, <nav>, <article>, and <footer>. They improve readability and SEO.
6. What is the difference between id and class?
An id is unique to one element. A class can be shared by many elements. CSS targets id with # and class with a dot.
7. What are attributes in HTML?
Extra information added to elements, written as name-value pairs in the opening tag, such as <img src="logo.png" alt="Logo">.
8. What is the difference between the alt and title attributes?
alt provides alternative text for images when they cannot load and aids accessibility. title shows a tooltip on hover.
9. How do you create a hyperlink?
Use the anchor tag: <a href="https://example.com">Visit</a>.
10. What is the difference between ordered and unordered lists?
<ol> creates a numbered list; <ul> creates a bulleted list. Both contain <li> items.
11. How do you insert an image?
Use the self-closing <img> tag with src and alt attributes.
12. What are the main elements of a table?
<table>, <tr> (row), <th> (header cell), and <td> (data cell).
13. What is the difference between GET and POST in forms?
GET appends data to the URL and is for non-sensitive requests. POST sends data in the request body and is for sensitive or large data.
14. What are common form input types?
text, password, email, number, radio, checkbox, date, file, and submit.
15. What is the difference between the action and method form attributes?
action sets the URL where the form data is sent; method sets how it is sent (GET or POST).
16. What does the required attribute do?
It marks a form field as mandatory, so the browser blocks submission until it is filled.
17. What is the difference between div and span?
<div> is a block-level container; <span> is an inline container for small pieces of content.
18. What is the purpose of the doctype declaration?
It tells the browser which version of HTML the page uses so it renders in standards mode.
19. What is the difference between the head and body sections?
The <head> holds metadata, titles, and links to styles and scripts. The <body> holds the visible page content.
20. What are void or self-closing elements?
Elements with no closing tag and no content, such as <br>, <hr>, <img>, and <input>.
21. What is the difference between local storage and session storage?
Both store data in the browser. Local storage persists until cleared; session storage lasts only for the browser tab session.

8. CSS

1. What is CSS?
CSS (Cascading Style Sheets) describes how HTML elements look, controlling colors, fonts, spacing, and layout.
2. What are the three ways to apply CSS?
Inline (in the element's style attribute), internal (in a <style> tag), and external (in a separate .css file linked to the page).
3. What is the difference between class and id selectors?
A class selector uses a dot (.name) and can apply to many elements. An id selector uses a hash (#name) and targets one unique element.
4. What is the CSS box model?
Every element is a box made of content, padding, border, and margin, from inside out.
5. What is the difference between padding and margin?
Padding is space inside an element, between content and border. Margin is space outside the border, between elements.
6. What is specificity in CSS?
A ranking that decides which rule wins when several target the same element. Inline styles beat ids, which beat classes, which beat element selectors.
7. What is the difference between relative, absolute, and fixed positioning?
  • relative: positioned relative to its normal place.
  • absolute: positioned relative to the nearest positioned ancestor.
  • fixed: positioned relative to the viewport and stays on scroll.
8. What is the difference between display none and visibility hidden?
display:none removes the element and its space. visibility:hidden hides it but keeps its space.
9. What is Flexbox?
A one-dimensional layout system that arranges items in a row or column and makes alignment and spacing easy.
10. What is CSS Grid?
A two-dimensional layout system that arranges content into rows and columns at the same time.
11. What is the difference between Flexbox and Grid?
Flexbox is best for one-dimensional layouts (a row or a column). Grid is best for two-dimensional layouts (rows and columns together).
12. What are pseudo-classes?
Keywords that style an element in a special state, such as :hover, :focus, and :first-child.
13. What is the difference between em and rem units?
em is relative to the parent element's font size; rem is relative to the root element's font size, making it more predictable.
14. What is the difference between absolute and relative units?
Absolute units (px, pt) are fixed. Relative units (em, rem, %, vw, vh) scale with context, which helps responsive design.
15. What is a media query?
A CSS rule that applies styles based on screen conditions like width, used to make pages responsive across devices.
16. What does the z-index property do?
It controls the stacking order of overlapping elements. A higher z-index appears in front.
17. What is the difference between inline, inline-block, and block?
Inline flows in text and ignores width/height. Block takes full width on a new line. Inline-block flows inline but accepts width and height.
18. How do you center an element horizontally?
For block elements, use margin: 0 auto with a set width. With Flexbox, use justify-content: center on the container.
19. What is the difference between absolute and relative paths in CSS links?
An absolute path gives the full URL. A relative path is based on the current file's location.
20. What does the cascade mean in CSS?
When multiple rules apply, the final style is decided by importance, specificity, and source order.
21. What are CSS variables?
Custom properties defined with --name and used with var(--name), allowing reusable values like colors across the stylesheet.

9. JavaScript

1. What is JavaScript?
A scripting language that adds interactivity and dynamic behavior to web pages, running in the browser.
2. What is the difference between var, let, and const?
  • var: function-scoped, can be redeclared.
  • let: block-scoped, can be reassigned.
  • const: block-scoped, cannot be reassigned.
3. What are the data types in JavaScript?
Primitives: string, number, boolean, null, undefined, symbol, and bigint. Plus the object type for collections and functions.
4. What is the difference between == and ===?
== compares values after type conversion. === compares both value and type, which is safer.
5. What is the difference between null and undefined?
undefined means a variable was declared but not assigned. null is an intentional empty value set by the developer.
6. What is hoisting?
JavaScript's behavior of moving variable and function declarations to the top of their scope before execution.
7. What is a closure?
A function that remembers variables from the scope where it was created, even after that scope has finished running.
8. What is the difference between function declaration and function expression?
A declaration is hoisted and can be called before it appears. An expression is assigned to a variable and is not hoisted as a callable.
9. What is an arrow function?
A shorter function syntax using =>. It does not have its own this and inherits it from the surrounding scope.
10. What is the difference between synchronous and asynchronous code?
Synchronous code runs line by line and blocks. Asynchronous code (callbacks, promises, async/await) lets long tasks run without blocking the rest.
11. What is a promise?
An object representing the eventual result of an asynchronous operation, with states pending, fulfilled, or rejected.
12. What is async/await?
Syntax that makes asynchronous code read like synchronous code. await pauses until a promise resolves inside an async function.
13. What is the DOM?
The Document Object Model is a tree representation of the page that JavaScript can read and modify to change content and structure.
14. How do you select elements in the DOM?
With methods like getElementById, querySelector, and querySelectorAll.
15. What is event bubbling?
When an event on an element travels up to its ancestors, triggering their handlers too. It can be stopped with stopPropagation().
16. What is the difference between map, filter, and forEach?
  • map: returns a new array of transformed values.
  • filter: returns a new array of items that pass a test.
  • forEach: loops over items but returns nothing.
17. What is the difference between an array and an object?
An array stores ordered values accessed by index. An object stores data as key-value pairs accessed by key.
18. What does the this keyword refer to?
It refers to the object that is calling the current function, which depends on how the function is invoked.
19. What is JSON?
JavaScript Object Notation, a lightweight text format for exchanging data, using key-value pairs and arrays.
20. What is the difference between local storage, session storage, and cookies?
All store data in the browser. Local storage persists, session storage lasts per tab, and cookies are small and sent with HTTP requests.
21. What is the difference between undefined and not defined?
undefined means a declared variable has no value. "Not defined" is a reference error for a variable that was never declared.

10. Bootstrap 5

1. What is Bootstrap?
Bootstrap is a popular front-end CSS framework that provides ready-made components and a responsive grid to build websites quickly.
2. What are the advantages of Bootstrap?
It is responsive, saves development time, ensures consistency, works across browsers, and offers many reusable components.
3. What is new in Bootstrap 5?
It dropped jQuery, added new utilities, improved the grid with an xxl breakpoint, and introduced custom CSS variables.
4. How do you add Bootstrap to a project?
Either link the Bootstrap CDN in the HTML head, or install it through a package manager and import the CSS and JS files.
5. What is the Bootstrap grid system?
A responsive 12-column layout built with flexbox, using containers, rows, and columns to arrange content.
6. What is the difference between container and container-fluid?
container has a fixed responsive width with margins. container-fluid spans the full width of the viewport.
7. Why must grid columns add up to 12?
The grid divides each row into 12 units. Columns summing to 12 fit on one line; more than 12 wraps to a new line.
8. What are the responsive breakpoints in Bootstrap 5?
sm (576px), md (768px), lg (992px), xl (1200px), and xxl (1400px), with extra-small as the default below 576px.
9. What does col-12 col-md-6 mean?
The column is full width on small screens and half width from the medium breakpoint upward. This is mobile-first design.
10. What are Bootstrap utility classes?
Small single-purpose classes for spacing, color, display, and alignment, such as m-2, p-3, and text-center.
11. How do you make an element a flex container?
Add the d-flex class. Combine it with direction and alignment classes as needed.
12. How do you center content with Bootstrap flex utilities?
Use d-flex justify-content-center for horizontal centering and align-items-center for vertical centering.
13. What are Bootstrap spacing utilities?
Classes for margin (m) and padding (p) with a side and size, such as mt-3 (margin-top) and px-2 (horizontal padding).
14. What are Bootstrap cards?
Flexible content containers with optional headers, bodies, footers, and images, used to display grouped information.
15. How do you create a button in Bootstrap?
Use the btn class plus a style class, for example <button class="btn btn-primary">Save</button>.
16. What is a Bootstrap navbar?
A responsive navigation header component that can collapse into a toggle menu on small screens.
17. What is a Bootstrap modal?
A dialog box that appears on top of the page content, used for forms, alerts, or confirmations.
18. How does Bootstrap support responsive design?
Through its mobile-first grid, responsive breakpoints, and utilities that adjust layout and visibility per screen size.
19. What are display utilities in Bootstrap?
Classes like d-none, d-block, and d-md-flex that show, hide, or change an element's display at specific breakpoints.
20. How do you hide an element on small screens only?
Use d-none d-md-block, which hides the element by default and shows it from the medium breakpoint upward.
21. What is the difference between Bootstrap and CSS?
CSS is the core styling language. Bootstrap is a framework built on CSS that provides predefined classes and components for faster development.

11. Agile

1. What is Agile?
Agile is an iterative approach to software development that delivers work in small increments with continuous feedback and flexibility to change.
2. What is the difference between Agile and Waterfall?
Waterfall is sequential and rigid. Agile is iterative and flexible, delivering working software frequently and adapting to change.
3. What is Scrum?
Scrum is the most popular Agile framework. It organizes work into sprints with defined roles, events, and artifacts.
4. What is a sprint?
A fixed-length iteration, usually one to four weeks, in which the team delivers a usable increment of the product.
5. What are the roles in Scrum?
Product Owner, Scrum Master, and the Development Team.
6. What does the Product Owner do?
Owns and prioritizes the product backlog and represents the customer to maximize product value.
7. What does the Scrum Master do?
Facilitates the Scrum process, removes impediments, and coaches the team, without acting as a traditional manager.
8. What are the main Scrum ceremonies?
Sprint Planning, Daily Standup, Sprint Review, and Sprint Retrospective.
9. What happens in Sprint Planning?
The team selects backlog items for the sprint and defines the sprint goal and plan.
10. What is the Daily Standup?
A short daily meeting, about 15 minutes, where members share progress, plans, and blockers.
11. What are the three Daily Standup questions?
What did I do yesterday? What will I do today? Are there any blockers?
12. What happens in the Sprint Review?
The team demonstrates the completed increment to stakeholders and collects feedback.
13. What is the Sprint Retrospective?
A meeting where the team reflects on the process and agrees on improvements for the next sprint.
14. What is a product backlog?
An ordered list of all desired features, fixes, and work for the product, maintained by the Product Owner.
15. What is a sprint backlog?
The set of backlog items chosen for the current sprint plus the plan to complete them.
16. What is a user story?
A short, simple description of a feature from the user's perspective: "As a user, I want to ... so that ...".
17. What are story points?
A relative measure of the effort and complexity of a user story, often estimated using Fibonacci numbers.
18. What is velocity?
The average amount of work, in story points, that a team completes per sprint, used for forecasting.
19. What is the Definition of Done?
A shared checklist of conditions a backlog item must meet to be considered complete, such as coded, tested, and reviewed.
20. What is a burndown chart?
A graph showing remaining work against time in a sprint, helping the team track progress toward the goal.
21. What is the difference between Scrum and Kanban?
Scrum works in fixed sprints with defined roles. Kanban is a continuous flow that visualizes work and limits work in progress, without sprints.

12. Software Engineering

1. What is software engineering?
A disciplined approach to designing, building, testing, and maintaining software using engineering principles to ensure quality and reliability.
2. What is the Software Development Life Cycle?
The SDLC is the process of building software through stages: requirements, design, implementation, testing, deployment, and maintenance.
3. What are common SDLC models?
Waterfall, Iterative, Spiral, V-Model, and Agile.
4. What does SOLID stand for?
Single Responsibility, Open/Closed, Liskov Substitution, Interface Segregation, and Dependency Inversion.
5. What is the Single Responsibility Principle?
A class should have only one reason to change, meaning it should do only one job.
6. What is the Open/Closed Principle?
Software should be open for extension but closed for modification, so new behavior is added without changing existing code.
7. What is the Dependency Inversion Principle?
High-level modules should depend on abstractions, not concrete classes, which reduces coupling and improves testability.
8. What is a design pattern?
A reusable, proven solution to a common software design problem.
9. What are the three categories of design patterns?
Creational, Structural, and Behavioral.
10. What is the Singleton pattern?
A creational pattern that ensures a class has only one instance and provides a global access point to it.
11. What is the Factory pattern?
A creational pattern that creates objects without exposing the creation logic, returning a type based on input.
12. What is the difference between cohesion and coupling?
Cohesion is how focused a module is on one task (high is good). Coupling is how dependent modules are on each other (low is good).
13. What is UML?
Unified Modeling Language, a standard visual notation for designing and documenting software systems.
14. What is a class diagram?
A UML diagram that shows classes, their fields and methods, and the relationships between them.
15. What is a sequence diagram?
A UML diagram that shows how objects interact over time through an ordered series of messages.
16. What is the difference between aggregation and composition?
Aggregation is a weak whole-part where parts can exist alone. Composition is a strong whole-part where parts cannot exist without the whole.
17. What is functional versus non-functional requirement?
A functional requirement defines what the system does. A non-functional requirement defines qualities like performance, security, and usability.
18. What is technical debt?
The future cost of choosing a quick solution now instead of a better approach, which must be fixed later.
19. What is refactoring?
Improving the internal structure of code without changing its external behavior, to make it cleaner and easier to maintain.
20. What is the DRY principle?
Do not Repeat Yourself: avoid duplicating code by extracting common logic into reusable methods or classes.
21. What is the KISS principle?
Keep It Simple, Stupid: favor simple, clear solutions over unnecessary complexity.

13. Project Explanation (Spring MVC)

1. Can you explain your Spring MVC project?
Describe a user authentication and management web application built with Spring MVC, Thymeleaf, and MySQL. Users register and log in; based on their role they access different dashboards. Mention your role, the layers, and the outcome.
2. What is Spring MVC?
Spring MVC is a web framework in the Spring ecosystem that follows the Model-View-Controller pattern to build web applications in a structured way.
3. What is the MVC pattern?
  • Model: the data and business logic.
  • View: the user interface (Thymeleaf templates).
  • Controller: handles requests and connects model and view.
4. What is the DispatcherServlet?
The front controller in Spring MVC. It receives every HTTP request and routes it to the correct controller, then returns the response.
5. What is the role of the controller?
It handles incoming requests, calls the service layer, puts data into the model, and returns a view name to render.
6. What is the difference between @Controller and @RestController?
@Controller returns view names for web pages. @RestController returns data directly as JSON or text for REST APIs.
7. What is the difference between @GetMapping and @PostMapping?
@GetMapping handles GET requests for reading data or showing pages. @PostMapping handles POST requests for submitting data.
8. What is the service layer?
A layer that holds the business logic. The controller calls it, keeping logic separate from request handling.
9. What is the repository layer?
The data access layer that talks to the database, usually built with Spring Data JPA interfaces.
10. What is Spring Data JPA?
A Spring module that simplifies database access by providing ready-made repository interfaces, removing most boilerplate SQL code.
11. What is an entity?
A Java class annotated with @Entity that maps to a database table, where each field maps to a column.
12. What is Thymeleaf?
A server-side Java template engine that generates HTML pages by merging model data into templates.
13. How does data flow in your project?
Browser to controller, controller to service, service to repository, repository to MySQL, and the result returns through the model to a Thymeleaf view rendered as HTML.
14. What is dependency injection?
A design pattern where Spring supplies an object's dependencies automatically rather than the object creating them, which reduces coupling.
15. What is the difference between @Autowired and constructor injection?
@Autowired can inject into fields or setters. Constructor injection passes dependencies through the constructor and is the recommended approach.
16. How did you connect to MySQL?
By setting the datasource URL, username, and password in application.properties and adding the MySQL driver dependency, with JPA managing the tables.
17. How did you handle user authentication?
Using Spring Security with form login, a custom UserDetailsService that loads users from MySQL, and BCrypt to hash and verify passwords.
18. How did you implement role-based access?
By configuring URL rules in the security configuration so each role can access only its allowed pages, and denying others with a 403 error.
19. What is application.properties used for?
A central configuration file for settings like the database connection, server port, JPA behavior, and logging levels.
20. How did you handle errors in the project?
By using exception handling with @ControllerAdvice and custom error pages, and by validating user input before processing.
21. What would you improve in your project?
Add more automated tests, introduce REST APIs for a front-end framework, improve validation and security, and add features like password reset and pagination.

14. Logging

1. What is logging?
Logging is recording messages about what an application does at runtime, used for monitoring, debugging, and auditing.
2. Why is logging important?
It helps developers understand application behavior, diagnose problems in production, track errors, and audit activity without a debugger.
3. Why is logging better than System.out.println?
  • It supports severity levels.
  • It can be turned on or off through configuration.
  • It can write to files and other destinations with timestamps and context.
4. What are the common logging levels?
TRACE, DEBUG, INFO, WARN, ERROR, and FATAL, in increasing order of severity.
5. What is the difference between DEBUG and INFO?
DEBUG provides detailed diagnostic information for developers. INFO records high-level milestones of normal operation.
6. What is the difference between WARN and ERROR?
WARN signals an unexpected but recoverable situation. ERROR signals a failure in the current operation that needs attention.
7. If the level is set to WARN, which messages appear?
Only WARN, ERROR, and FATAL. Lower levels like INFO and DEBUG are suppressed.
8. What is an appender?
A component that decides where log output goes, such as the console, a file, a database, or email.
9. What is a console appender?
An appender that writes log messages to the console or standard output.
10. What is a rolling file appender?
An appender that writes to a file and starts a new file when the current one reaches a size limit or a time period.
11. What is a log layout or pattern?
A format that defines how each log line looks, including timestamp, level, logger name, thread, and message.
12. Can a logger use multiple appenders?
Yes. A logger can send the same messages to several destinations at once, such as console and file.
13. What is a logger?
An object used in code to create log messages, usually created per class to identify the message source.
14. What are popular logging frameworks in Java?
Log4j 2, Logback, and the SLF4J facade, plus the built-in java.util.logging.
15. What is parameterized logging?
Using placeholders like log.info("User {} logged in", id) so the message string is built only when that level is enabled.
16. Why is parameterized logging preferred?
It avoids unnecessary string concatenation when the level is disabled, improving performance.
17. What is the FATAL level?
The most severe level, used for critical failures that may cause the application to stop. It exists in Log4j.
18. How do you configure logging in Spring Boot?
Through application.properties for levels, or by adding a logback or log4j2 configuration file for advanced control.
19. What is log rotation?
Automatically archiving old log files and starting new ones to prevent a single file from growing too large.
20. What should you avoid logging?
Sensitive data such as passwords, card numbers, and personal information, for security and privacy.
21. What is the benefit of log levels in production?
You can run at INFO or WARN in production and increase to DEBUG temporarily to investigate issues, all without changing code.

15. SLF4J

1. What is SLF4J?
SLF4J (Simple Logging Facade for Java) is an abstraction layer that provides a common API for various logging frameworks.
2. What does facade mean in SLF4J?
A facade is a single, simple interface in front of different implementations. You code against SLF4J, and it delegates to the real logging framework.
3. Why use SLF4J instead of a logging framework directly?
It lets you switch the underlying framework (Logback, Log4j 2) without changing application code, avoiding vendor lock-in.
4. Is SLF4J a logging framework?
No. SLF4J is only an API or facade. It needs a real implementation like Logback or Log4j 2 behind it to actually log.
5. How do you create a logger with SLF4J?
Use LoggerFactory.getLogger(MyClass.class) to get a Logger, typically stored in a private static final field.
6. What is the typical import for SLF4J?
org.slf4j.Logger and org.slf4j.LoggerFactory.
7. What logging methods does SLF4J provide?
Methods named after levels: trace(), debug(), info(), warn(), and error().
8. Does SLF4J support placeholders?
Yes. It supports the {} placeholder, for example log.info("Order {} created", id).
9. What is the advantage of SLF4J placeholders?
The final message is built only if the level is active, which improves performance and keeps code clean.
10. What is a binding in SLF4J?
A binding is the adapter that connects SLF4J to a specific logging implementation, such as the Logback or Log4j 2 binding.
11. What happens if no binding is found?
SLF4J prints a warning that no implementation was found and discards log messages with a no-operation logger.
12. Can you have multiple bindings?
You should have only one. Multiple bindings cause a warning, and SLF4J picks one unpredictably.
13. What is the difference between SLF4J and Logback?
SLF4J is the API. Logback is an implementation that natively supports SLF4J and does the actual logging work.
14. Does Spring Boot use SLF4J?
Yes. Spring Boot uses SLF4J as the logging facade with Logback as the default implementation.
15. How do you log an exception with SLF4J?
Pass the exception as the last argument, for example log.error("Failed for {}", id, e), to print the stack trace.
16. Why is coding against an interface like SLF4J good practice?
It decouples your code from a specific library, making the application flexible, maintainable, and easy to change later.
17. What is the MDC in SLF4J?
Mapped Diagnostic Context, a per-thread map for adding contextual data like a user ID or request ID to every log line.
18. Can SLF4J work with Log4j?
Yes. With the proper SLF4J-to-Log4j binding, SLF4J calls are routed to Log4j as the implementation.
19. Is SLF4J dependent on a specific framework?
No. That independence is its main purpose. It works with any supported logging backend.
20. What are the benefits of SLF4J in large projects?
Consistent logging API across modules, easy framework switching, better performance with placeholders, and cleaner code.
21. What is the simplest SLF4J usage example?
private static final Logger log =
    LoggerFactory.getLogger(MyService.class);

log.info("Service started");
log.error("Something failed", exception);

16. Log4j and SLF4J Together

1. How do Log4j and SLF4J work together?
You write code against the SLF4J API, and Log4j 2 acts as the implementation behind it through a binding adapter. SLF4J is the front; Log4j does the work.
2. What is the main difference between SLF4J and Log4j?
SLF4J is a facade or API. Log4j is a concrete logging framework. SLF4J defines how to log; Log4j performs the logging.
3. Why combine SLF4J with Log4j?
To get a flexible, vendor-neutral API (SLF4J) while using the powerful features and configuration of Log4j 2 underneath.
4. What is the role of the binding between them?
The binding is an adapter library that routes SLF4J calls to Log4j, so the two can communicate.
5. Is Log4j an implementation or a facade?
Log4j is an implementation. It can also be used directly, but it is commonly paired behind the SLF4J facade.
6. Can you use Log4j without SLF4J?
Yes. Log4j has its own API and can be used alone, but using it through SLF4J is more flexible.
7. Can you use SLF4J without Log4j?
Yes. SLF4J can run with other implementations such as Logback or java.util.logging.
8. Which is better, Log4j or SLF4J?
It is not either-or. SLF4J is the recommended API to code against, and Log4j 2 or Logback is the implementation. They complement each other.
9. What is Log4j 2?
The modern version of Log4j, offering better performance, asynchronous logging, and flexible configuration over the original Log4j 1.
10. How is Log4j 2 configured?
Through a configuration file such as log4j2.xml, which defines appenders, layouts, and logger levels.
11. What does a typical Log4j 2 configuration contain?
Appenders (console, file), pattern layouts for formatting, and loggers with levels and appender references.
12. What is the recommended logging setup in modern Java?
Code against SLF4J and use Logback or Log4j 2 as the backend, keeping the application independent of the implementation.
13. How do SLF4J levels map to Log4j levels?
They align directly: TRACE, DEBUG, INFO, WARN, and ERROR map one to one between the two.
14. What happens if both Logback and Log4j bindings are present?
SLF4J warns about multiple bindings and uses one unpredictably. Keep only one implementation on the classpath.
15. What is the advantage of this layered approach?
You can swap the logging backend without touching application code, since all logging calls go through SLF4J.
16. What is asynchronous logging in Log4j 2?
A feature that logs messages on a separate thread, improving performance under heavy load.
17. What was the Log4Shell vulnerability?
A serious security flaw in older Log4j 2 versions. The fix is to upgrade to a patched version, which is an important best practice.
18. How do you write a log statement in this setup?
The same SLF4J way: get a logger from LoggerFactory and call log.info(...). Log4j handles output behind the scenes.
19. Does the application code change when switching backends?
No. With SLF4J, only the dependencies and configuration change, not the logging code in your classes.
20. What is the benefit for a team using SLF4J plus Log4j?
A consistent API for all developers, powerful configuration from Log4j, and freedom to change the backend later.
21. Summarize the relationship in one line.
SLF4J is the standard interface you program to, and Log4j 2 is the engine that carries out the logging behind it.

17. General Interview Questions

1. What is the difference between JDK, JRE, and JVM?
  • JVM: runs Java bytecode.
  • JRE: JVM plus libraries to run programs.
  • JDK: JRE plus tools to develop programs.
2. What is the difference between a compiler and an interpreter?
A compiler translates the whole program before running. An interpreter translates and runs line by line. Java uses both: it compiles to bytecode, then interprets and JIT-compiles it.
3. What is the difference between an API and a library?
A library is reusable code. An API is the set of methods and rules that define how to interact with that code or a service.
4. What is the difference between a database and a DBMS?
A database is an organized collection of data. A DBMS is the software, like MySQL, used to manage and access that data.
5. What is the difference between SQL and NoSQL?
SQL databases are relational with fixed schemas and tables. NoSQL databases are non-relational and flexible, storing documents, key-values, or graphs.
6. What is a primary key?
A column or set of columns that uniquely identifies each row in a table and cannot be null.
7. What is a foreign key?
A column that links to the primary key of another table, enforcing a relationship between the two.
8. What is the difference between a primary key and a unique key?
A table has one primary key that cannot be null. It can have many unique keys, which allow a single null depending on the database.
9. What is normalization?
Organizing database tables to reduce redundancy and improve data integrity by splitting data into related tables.
10. What is a join in SQL?
An operation that combines rows from two or more tables based on a related column, such as inner join and left join.
11. What is a REST API?
An architectural style for web services that uses HTTP methods like GET, POST, PUT, and DELETE to work with resources.
12. What are common HTTP status codes?
200 OK, 201 Created, 400 Bad Request, 401 Unauthorized, 403 Forbidden, 404 Not Found, and 500 Internal Server Error.
13. What is the difference between GET and POST?
GET retrieves data and sends parameters in the URL. POST sends data in the request body, suitable for creating or sensitive data.
14. What is the difference between authentication and authorization?
Authentication verifies who you are. Authorization decides what you are allowed to do.
15. What is an IDE?
An Integrated Development Environment, like IntelliJ IDEA or Eclipse, that provides editing, building, debugging, and other tools in one place.
16. What is the difference between debugging and testing?
Testing finds whether defects exist. Debugging locates and fixes the cause of a known defect.
17. What is an algorithm?
A step-by-step set of instructions to solve a problem or perform a task.
18. What is the difference between time and space complexity?
Time complexity measures how runtime grows with input size. Space complexity measures how memory use grows with input size.
19. What is Big O notation?
A way to describe the worst-case growth rate of an algorithm, such as O(1), O(n), or O(n squared).
20. What is the difference between a stack and a queue?
A stack is last-in-first-out. A queue is first-in-first-out.
21. What is the difference between frontend and backend?
The frontend is the user-facing part in the browser. The backend is the server-side logic and database that powers the application.
